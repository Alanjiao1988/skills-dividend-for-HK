# Industry and Sector Analysis

## Purpose and Position in the Research Framework

Industry analysis is a mandatory part of every Full Analysis. It is not an optional background paragraph and cannot be replaced by company management's market-size claims.

Read this module together with `industry-analysis.md`, `historical-financial-trends.md`, `business-fundamentals.md`, `business-outlook.md`, and `sector-fcf-proxies.md`.

The framework has three distinct investment layers:

1. **Dividend income value:** cash distributions, tax-adjusted income, recurring distributable cash, capital and funding constraints.
2. **Business growth value:** industry economics, company positioning, segment growth, reinvestment returns and per-share operating growth.
3. **Valuation and total return:** the price paid for both income and growth, scenario value, dividends received and terminal capital value.

Industry research primarily informs Layer 2, but it must flow into cash generation, dividend capacity and Layer 3 valuation. A favourable industry outlook does not automatically make a company attractive.

## 1. Identify the Industry Before Selecting the Model

For every issuer, establish and disclose:

- Primary industry and sub-industry, using a recognized classification where available (GICS, ICB, exchange/regulatory classification or a clearly named local equivalent).
- Actual business model and position in the value chain: producer, brand owner, distributor, platform, network operator, asset owner, service provider, financier, holding company, or a combination.
- Main operating geographies, customer types, product/service lines and revenue currencies.
- Segment contribution to revenue, operating profit/EBITDA, assets, capital employed and cash generation, where disclosed.
- Economic characteristics: cyclical/defensive, regulated/unregulated, capital-intensive/asset-light, commodity-exposed, network-effect, concession-based, subscription-based or project-based.
- Any material secondary industry exposure. Do not force a diversified issuer into a single-sector model if segments have materially different economics.

Use issuer filings and exchange disclosures as the primary classification evidence. If official classifications conflict with the actual revenue/profit mix, state both and explain which operating model drives the analysis.

For diversified groups, perform a segment-level industry analysis and then reconcile segment findings to the consolidated group. A conglomerate label is not an adequate sector analysis.

## 2. Industry Economics: Mandatory Questions

Answer each question with dated evidence and explain the consequence for the issuer:

1. **Demand:** What drives end-market volume, customer count, utilization or consumption? Is demand growing, stable or structurally declining?
2. **Supply and capacity:** What capacity is available, under construction or likely to exit? Are there entry barriers, long lead times or excess capacity?
3. **Price and mix:** Who has pricing power? Can higher prices offset lower volumes, inflation or customer churn? Separate price, volume, mix and FX.
4. **Profit pool:** Which value-chain participants capture the economics? Where are gross margin, operating margin and returns on capital strongest?
5. **Competition:** Market shares, concentration, capacity additions, substitutes, switching costs, distribution access and competitor strategies.
6. **Technology and substitution:** Which product, process or channel changes can expand, displace or commoditize the issuer's business?
7. **Regulation and policy:** Licensing, spectrum, concessions, taxes/excise, tariffs, price controls, environmental rules, safety, sanctions, reimbursement and litigation.
8. **Capital intensity:** Maintenance versus growth investment, working capital, inventory, regulatory capital and the time between investment and cash generation.
9. **Industry cycle:** Current position in the supply, inventory, credit, freight-rate, commodity or replacement cycle. Explain the cycle-specific normalization window.
10. **External exposures:** Interest rates, FX, commodity inputs, fuel, energy, freight, trade restrictions and geopolitical concentration.

Do not substitute a global TAM/CAGR for company-specific analysis. Translate industry growth into a plausible issuer outcome using market share, capacity, customer access, pricing, margins, investment and funding assumptions.

## 3. Competitive Structure and Peer Selection

Select normally three to five listed peers. Use the closest available combination of sub-industry, business model, geography, product mix and accounting period.

For each peer, document:
- Why it is comparable and the most important limitation.
- Revenue/operating growth, operating margin, return on capital or the sector's equivalent.
- Net debt/leverage or regulatory capital, cash conversion and dividend capacity where meaningful.
- Valuation using comparable definitions and the same market-price date.
- Competitive position: scale, cost curve, brand, network, customer access, technology, route/asset quality, regulatory access or distribution.

Do not force a peer set when no genuinely comparable issuer exists. Use a clearly labelled adjacent benchmark or industry aggregate and explain the difference. Never imply that a peer is directly comparable merely because it shares a broad sector label.

Peer comparisons must use consistent periods, currencies, consolidation perimeters and metric definitions. Mark unavailable values rather than mixing EBITDA, EBIT, adjusted EBIT, FFO or regulatory capital as if interchangeable.

## 4. Sector Playbooks and Required Operating KPIs

Choose the relevant playbook(s) below. The listed KPIs are a minimum starting set, not a requirement to invent unavailable disclosures. Explain which metrics are reported, estimated, unavailable or not economically relevant.

| Industry / business model | Core operating and industry KPIs | Financial transmission to equity value |
|---|---|---|
| Tobacco / nicotine | Combustible volume decline, price/mix, illicit trade, excise, market share, adult consumers/users, new-category revenue, device/consumable attachment, category contribution margin, regulatory market access | Legacy cash harvest versus substitution, new-category growth and margin ramp, excise/tax burden, litigation and stranded investment |
| Telecom | Mobile/fixed subscribers, net adds, ARPU, churn, data usage, service revenue, EBITDA margin, capex/revenue, spectrum spending, 5G/fibre penetration, net debt | Network investment cycle, pricing competition, recurring cash flow, spectrum/concession obligations and dividend cover |
| Shipping / ocean freight | Freight rates by route/type, TCE/day rates, cargo volumes, fleet utilization, fleet age, orderbook, scrapping, spot/contract mix, charter coverage, bunker cost | Freight-rate cycle, operating leverage, vessel capex, charter commitments, balance-sheet resilience and variable distributions |
| Ports / terminals | Throughput/TEU, container mix, tariff/yield, utilization, capacity, concession duration, transshipment share, shipping-line concentration, capex | Volume and tariff growth, concession economics, incremental ROIC, maintenance/growth capex and remittable cash |
| Oil & gas / energy | Production, reserves and reserve life, decline rate, realized prices, lifting cost, breakeven, hedges, refining/marketing margins, sustaining capex | Commodity-price sensitivity, depletion, project returns, cash breakeven, leverage and cycle-normalized distributions |
| Banks | Loan/deposit growth, NIM, NII, deposit beta, fee income, NPL, credit cost, coverage, CET1, ROTE/ROTCE, liquidity | Credit cycle, capital generation, regulatory payout limits and P/TBV or residual-income valuation; do not use industrial FCF |
| Insurance | Premium growth, retention, combined ratio, investment spread, VNB, CSM, claims development, solvency/capital generation | Underwriting quality, reserve risk, capital release, reinvestment yield and distributable remittances |
| REITs / property | Occupancy, same-store NOI, rent reversion, WALE, tenant concentration, FFO/AFFO, cap rate, LTV, debt maturity and interest cover | Property cash yield, refinancing cost, asset valuation, recurring capex and distribution coverage |
| Utilities | Rate base/RAB, allowed and earned ROE, tariff resets, generation mix, availability, capex pipeline, leverage and interest cover | Regulated return, project funding, rate-case lag, dilution and dividend coverage |
| Consumer staples / branded products | Volume, price/mix, market share, distribution, sell-through, repeat purchase, gross margin, marketing intensity and inventory | Brand/pricing power, elasticity, channel inventory, product mix and cash conversion |
| Industrials / manufacturing | Orders, backlog quality, book-to-bill, shipment, capacity utilization, yield/scrap, unit cost, inventory, receivable days, maintenance and growth capex | Order conversion, operating leverage, working-capital absorption, replacement cycle and incremental ROIC |
| SaaS / software / technology | ARR/MRR, net/gross revenue retention, churn, customer cohort, ACV, RPO/cRPO, gross margin, CAC payback, sales efficiency, SBC and FCF margin | Recurring growth quality, retention, acquisition efficiency, dilution and post-SBC free cash flow |
| Semiconductors / hardware | Units, ASP, product mix, node/technology, utilization, yield, inventory days, design wins, customer concentration, capacity and equipment capex | Semiconductor cycle, pricing/mix, yield ramp, inventory correction, capex intensity and customer dependency |
| Pharma / healthcare | Product sales, patient funnel, pricing/reimbursement, patent/LOE dates, pipeline milestones, clinical success probabilities, R&D, launch cost and cash runway | Product concentration, patent cliffs, risk-adjusted pipeline value, pricing pressure and R&D-to-cash conversion |
| Automotive / EV | Deliveries, ASP, model mix, unit contribution, utilization, warranty/recall cost, battery cost, order backlog and dealer inventory | Price competition, scale economics, capacity utilization, working capital and funding runway |
| Transport / logistics | Tonnage/parcels, load factor, yield per unit, network utilization, cost per shipment, fuel, labour, fleet capex and customer concentration | Volume/yield mix, cost absorption, network density, fuel sensitivity and maintenance investment |
| Asset managers / exchanges / market infrastructure | AUM/net flows, fee rate, trading volume, product mix, client assets, recurring data revenue, margin and regulatory capital | Market-level sensitivity, fee compression, operating leverage, client retention and capital-light cash generation |

For an industry not listed, derive a bespoke KPI set from the actual revenue and cash engines. State why those KPIs are economically causal. Do not apply an adjacent sector's metrics mechanically.

For banks, insurers, REITs, utilities and holding companies, combine this module with `sector-fcf-proxies.md`; that file remains the authority for distributable-cash and capital/remittance calculations.

## 5. Industry Outlook and Company-Specific Translation

Build a three-to-five-year industry view covering:

- Base, bear and bull demand/supply outcomes.
- Expected price, volume, utilization, market-share and margin direction.
- Competitive capacity, substitution, innovation and likely market-share transfers.
- Regulatory milestones and downside asymmetry.
- Required investment and likely returns for incumbents.
- What part of the industry's expected growth the issuer can realistically capture.

For each major driver, show the chain:

`Industry driver -> issuer volume/share/price/mix -> segment revenue -> segment margin/profit -> investment and working capital -> owner cash per share`.

Separate industry growth from company execution. A growing market can coexist with falling issuer share, lower margins or negative free cash flow. A declining industry can still support a cash-generative incumbent, but its finite-life harvest assumptions and terminal value must reflect decline.

## 6. Required Output in the Full Report

The full report must include:

1. Industry classification, business model, value-chain position and segment exposure.
2. Industry structure, demand/supply, cycle, regulation, technology/substitution and profit-pool analysis.
3. Three-to-five peers or a justified alternative benchmark, with comparable quantitative metrics.
4. Sector-specific KPIs and their five-year history where available.
5. A company-specific industry thesis with bear/base/bull outcomes.
6. At least three measurable industry/company monitoring indicators and thesis-invalidation thresholds.

Every major industry claim must have a dated source. Prefer issuer filings, regulators, exchange statistics and official industry datasets; use reputable industry research for context and identify estimates as such. Record publication date, data period, geography, units and source URL. A generic industry narrative without issuer-specific financial transmission does not satisfy this module.
