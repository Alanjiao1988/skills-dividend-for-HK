# Historical Financial Trends and Deterioration Analysis

## Purpose

Every Full Analysis must compare at least five completed fiscal years of meaningful financial and operating data, when available, and explain the direction, quality and causes of change. A latest-quarter or TTM snapshot is supplementary; it does not replace the five-year history.

Read with `industry-analysis.md`, `business-fundamentals.md`, `sector-fcf-proxies.md` and `data-conventions.md`. The purpose is not to fill a table with numbers. It is to identify what has improved, what has deteriorated, when the trend changed, and whether the change is temporary, cyclical, persistent or structural.

## 1. Historical Window and Comparability

- Use the latest five completed fiscal years (FY-4 through FY) where available. Show the actual fiscal year-end and do not silently mix calendar and fiscal years.
- Add latest TTM, interim or quarterly data in a separate column/table. Never compare a half-year flow directly with a full-year flow without annualization and an explicit label.
- Use the latest restated comparative figures where the issuer has retrospectively restated prior years. Flag restatements, accounting-policy changes, acquisitions/disposals, discontinued operations, currency translation and changes in consolidation perimeter.
- Keep reported and adjusted figures separate. Explain recurring versus non-recurring adjustments; do not treat management-adjusted metrics as reported GAAP/IFRS results.
- Use a full economic cycle for cyclical industries when five years do not cover the cycle. The five-year table remains useful but must not be mislabelled as mid-cycle.
- If five years are unavailable, show the full available period and state why. Never fabricate missing years or infer that unavailable data equals zero.

## 2. Mandatory Historical Financial Dataset

Select the sector-appropriate metrics, but the Full Report must cover each applicable category:

| Category | Core measures |
|---|---|
| Growth | Revenue/sector income, segment revenue, operating profit/EBITDA, net income, diluted EPS, FCF and FCF per share, DPS; 1-year change and 3-/5-year CAGR where meaningful |
| Profitability | Gross margin, operating/EBIT margin, EBITDA margin, net margin, segment margins, return on invested capital (ROIC) or sector equivalent |
| Cash generation | Operating cash flow, maintenance and growth capex when separable, reported FCF, normalized owner FCF/sector proxy, FCF margin, OCF/net income, FCF conversion |
| Working capital | Receivable, inventory and payable days; cash conversion cycle; contract assets/liabilities or deferred revenue where material |
| Balance sheet | Cash, gross debt, net debt, lease liabilities, net debt/EBITDA, interest coverage, debt maturity/refinancing needs, pension or other material claims |
| Per-share economics | Basic/diluted shares, EPS, FCF/share, DPS, share issuance, scrip/DRIP dilution, buybacks and net share-count change |
| Capital allocation | Cash dividends, buybacks, M&A, divestments, debt repayment, growth investment, maintenance investment and retained capital |
| Sector KPIs | Industry-specific operating drivers from `industry-analysis.md`; at least the measures that explain most of the revenue, margin and cash movement |

For banks, insurers, REITs, utilities and holding companies, replace inapplicable industrial metrics with the sector measures in `sector-fcf-proxies.md`. Do not manufacture EBITDA, industrial FCF or net debt/EBITDA where those metrics are not economically meaningful.

Present the core five-year table in the main report. Use separate tables when one table would become unreadable. A practical presentation is:
- Operating and income statement trends.
- Cash flow and balance-sheet trends.
- Per-share returns, dividend and capital allocation.
- Industry KPIs and segment mix.

For every table, state currency, units, fiscal period, reported/adjusted basis and data cutoff. Include source references at row, column or table level. Do not omit a material year simply to fit a short page.

## 3. Trend Calculations and Diagnostic Ratios

Calculate, where data permits:

- Revenue, operating profit, EPS, FCF, FCF/share and DPS CAGR over three and five years.
- Margin changes in percentage points, not just percentage growth.
- OCF/net income and FCF/net income; explain years when earnings are not converted into cash.
- Capex/revenue and maintenance versus growth investment.
- Net debt and leverage trajectory, interest coverage and refinancing wall.
- Working-capital absorption/release and its contribution to OCF.
- Diluted share-count change and the effect of issuance, scrip and buybacks on per-share growth.
- Dividend payout against earnings and, separately, recurring distributable cash; actual cash paid versus entitlement.
- Incremental ROIC/ROCE or the appropriate sector return on new investment, where measurable.

Show the formula or definition when the metric is not standard. Keep units and denominator consistent. Do not calculate CAGR across negative values or a zero base without a meaningful alternative explanation.

## 4. Deterioration and Anomaly Detection

Actively test, not merely mention, the following conditions:

| Signal | Required investigation | Potential implication |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue or segment income declines | Volume, price/mix, market share, FX, disposals, product substitution and industry cycle | Shrinking demand, loss of share or temporary translation effect |
| Operating profit falls faster than revenue | Gross margin, cost inflation, utilization, product mix, pricing and restructuring | Operating deleverage or weakening competitive position |
| Net income rises while OCF falls | Receivables, inventory, tax timing, provisions, non-cash gains and cash interest | Lower earnings quality and weaker dividend funding |
| OCF/net income or FCF conversion deteriorates | Working capital, capitalized expenditure, cash tax/interest and exceptional items | Accounting earnings may overstate distributable cash |
| FCF falls despite stable/increasing earnings | Capex, lease payments, working capital, litigation, pension and other owner claims | Dividend capacity may be weaker than payout ratios imply |
| Net debt rises or liquidity falls | Buybacks/dividends, M&A, investment, refinancing, FX and debt maturity profile | Less financial flexibility and greater refinancing risk |
| Interest coverage falls | Debt repricing, floating-rate exposure, bond maturities and lower EBITDA/earnings | Rising fixed-charge burden |
| Capex or working capital spikes | Project pipeline, replacement cycle, inventory build, receivable collection and supplier terms | Cash flow pressure; distinguish productive growth investment from leakage |
| EPS grows slower than total profit | Dilution, scrip, stock-based compensation and share issuance | Business growth is not reaching each share |
| DPS rises while cash coverage weakens | Payout policy, borrowing, asset sales, excess cash, scrip and exceptional receipts | Distribution may be unsupported or temporarily funded |
| ROIC/ROCE falls as investment rises | New-project economics, acquisitions, pricing, utilization and integration | Growth may destroy rather than create shareholder value |

For each material signal:
1. Identify the first year it appeared and whether it continued.
2. Quantify the change versus the prior year, five-year average/range and relevant peer/sector benchmark.
3. Decompose the cause into volume, price/mix, margin, FX, working capital, investment, financing, tax, acquisition/disposal and accounting effects.
4. Classify as **transient**, **cyclical**, **persistent** or **structural**.
5. Quantify the effect on owner cash, FAD, leverage, EPS/FCF per share, dividend capacity and valuation as relevant.
6. Define the next observable KPI, threshold, source and review date.

Do not use a universal percentage threshold for every sector. Set alert levels from the issuer's history, industry cycle, peer range, covenants, management guidance or disclosed commitments. A red flag must state the measured threshold and evidence; it cannot be a stylistic label.

## 5. Trend Status and Visual Presentation

Use a consistent status convention:
- **Red / material deterioration:** evidence of a breached covenant/commitment, structural cash impairment, persistent multi-year deterioration or a material dividend-funding shortfall.
- **Amber / watch:** a sustained adverse trend, deteriorating leading indicator or threshold approaching, but not yet a confirmed structural impairment.
- **Green / stable or improving:** evidence supports resilience or improvement; do not use green merely because data is missing.
- **Grey / unavailable:** insufficient comparable data. Missing evidence is not a pass.

The main report must explicitly call out material deterioration in revenue, operating profit, cash flow, FCF, leverage, interest cover, per-share metrics or industry KPIs. If a metric declines, state whether the decline is:
- a single-year timing issue;
- a cyclical trough or normalization;
- a multi-year persistent decline; or
- a structural deterioration that changes terminal economics.

Use charts for the most decision-relevant five-year series (normally revenue, operating profit, OCF/FCF, net debt/leverage and per-share indicators). A chart supplements rather than replaces the numerical table. Keep reported and normalized cash series visibly distinct.

## 6. Main-Report Placement

- Section 5, **过去五年经营与财务趋势**, contains the main five-year operating/financial tables and a clear deterioration assessment.
- Section 6, **现金流、资产负债与股息质量**, explains the cash conversion, leverage and dividend-funding consequences.
- Section 7, **企业成长性与资本配置**, explains reinvestment returns, incremental ROIC, dilution and per-share growth.
- Section 11, **风险、监测指标与论点证伪**, converts adverse trends into measurable watch thresholds and invalidation conditions.
- The appendix may carry full reconciliations and data-source detail, but it must not be the only place where a material five-year deterioration is disclosed.

## 7. Evidence and Integrity

Prioritize annual reports, interim reports, regulatory filings, exchange announcements, official industry datasets and audited segment disclosures. Use reputable market-data sources only for cross-checks or missing standardized series. For each material observation, record source URL, publication date, fiscal period, currency, units and whether reported, adjusted, reconstructed or estimated.

If two sources conflict, show the conflict and select the authoritative basis with an explanation. Do not silently overwrite original values. If data is unavailable after reasonable retrieval, show the gap and explain which conclusion it limits. A missing data point must never be filled with zero, an interpolated number or an unsupported consensus estimate.
